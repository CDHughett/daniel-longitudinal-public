It is a transition record.

It does not independently define the phase architecture.

[`PHASE_MAP.md`](PHASE_MAP.md)

Phase-declaration requirements are defined in:

# Purpose

This file exists to document:

- completed phase transitions
- the evidence windows supporting those transitions
- the distinction between formal declarations and operating substates
- the current undeclared transition boundary
- major structural milestones that changed system interpretation

Routine progress, short-term fluctuations, and isolated favorable observations are intentionally excluded.

---

# Transition Record Types

Entries in this file use the following classifications.

## Formal Phase Transition

A retrospectively declared movement from one canonical phase to another after the applicable criteria were satisfied.

A formal transition requires:

- completion of the previous phase
- supporting artifacts
- demonstrated repeatability
- structural change
- risk review
- retrospective documentation

---

## Operating-Substate Change

A meaningful change in how the active phase is expressed without creating a new canonical phase.

Examples include:

- entry
- reintegration
- consolidation
- lock-in observation
- testing-window preservation
- portability observation

An operating-substate change does not independently authorize phase advancement.

---

## Candidate Transition Evidence

An observation that may become relevant to a future declaration.

Examples include:

- reduced operator overhead
- ambient execution
- divided-attention control
- portability
- stable recovery across varied contexts
- trait-like movement expression

Candidate evidence is not a declared state.

---

## Reserved Transition

A future structural category that has not been activated.

A reserved transition is not:

- a prediction
- a scheduled progression
- an expectation
- an entitlement created by favorable results

# T-001 — Establishment of Phase 0

## Classification

Historical baseline establishment.

## State Entered

```text
Phase 0 — Baseline Reconstruction
```

## Evidence Window

Broadly represented by the pre-structured 2025 observation period.

The earliest public snapshot layers include:

- May 2025
- July 2025
- August 2025

Exact phase-entry timing is not retrospectively inferred beyond the preserved evidence.

## Structural Conditions

Phase 0 was characterized by:

- endurance-dominant activity
- environmental and heat exposure
- water-based conditioning
- sparse mechanical loading
- limited structured progression
- incomplete training architecture
- exploratory measurement
- recognition that high output alone was not producing the desired structural trajectory

The system moved from ordinary unstructured self-management into an explicitly observed baseline-reconstruction period.

The primary change was not a new performance capability.

It was the recognition that:

- the current trajectory required intervention
- observations needed to be preserved
- endurance volume and structured progression were not equivalent
- a governed architecture was necessary

# T-003 — Phase 1 to Phase 2

## Classification

Formal phase transition.

## From

```text
Phase 1 — Firmware Installation and Stabilization
```

## To

```text
Phase 2 — Load Integration
```

## Evidence Boundary

Phase 1 completion was supported by the late-2025 artifact set.

Phase 2 activation is represented in the February 2026 snapshot and the early 2026 weekly reporting layer.

Relevant archive anchors include:

- November 2025 — Phase 1 completion baseline
- February 2026 — Phase 2 activation snapshot
- 2026-W06 — early governed Phase 2 weekly reporting

The archive does not retroactively invent an exact transition day between those preserved windows.

## Phase 1 Completion Basis

Phase 1 was retrospectively treated as complete after evidence of:

- stabilized training cadence
- improved recovery consistency
- repeatable aerobic execution
- directional recomposition
- improved metabolic markers
- restored load tolerance
- synchronized multi-system improvement
- sufficient architecture to support mechanical integration

## Structural Shift

The system moved from:

```text
Installing and stabilizing the base architecture
                    ↓
Testing that architecture under recurring mechanical load
```

Phase 2 introduced or emphasized:

- trap-bar loading
- pull-ups
- push-ups and related upper-body loading
- structured strength exposure
- preserved B1 aerobic work
- recovery monitoring under combined modalities
- mechanical-signal observation
- repeated load without compensatory escalation

## Governing Interpretation

The transition did not mean that recovery work was complete or no longer relevant.

It meant that recovery stability had become sufficient to support a new primary question:

> Can structured mechanical load be integrated without destabilizing the aerobic and recovery platform?

# T-004 — Phase 2 Entry to Consolidation

## Classification

Operating-substate change.

## Canonical Phase

```text
Phase 2 — Load Integration
```

## From

```text
Early load integration / controlled expansion
```

## To

```text
Consolidation / lock-in observation
```

## Evidence Window

The shift developed across repeated Phase 2 weekly observation rather than through one isolated event.

Relevant evidence appears from:

- 2026-W06 — controlled expansion
- 2026-W07 — lock-in behavior observed
- 2026-W08 onward — consolidation monitoring
- later Phase 2 reports — recovery-floor durability, portability, and trait-level work-capacity observation

## Structural Change

The operating question shifted from:

> Can structured load be tolerated?

toward:

> Does integrated load remain stable, repeatable, low-friction, and compatible with ordinary life?

## Indicators

Observed indicators included:

- repeated B1 completion
- repeated Load Integration completion
- stable same-day compatibility
- recovery preserved after strength exposure
- decreasing perceived effort
- reduced need for graded re-entry
- increasing mechanical familiarity
- improved pull-up control
- automatic or ambient execution
- stable reintegration after disruption
- optional activity remaining subordinate
- preserved governance despite increasing capacity

## Lock-In Interpretation

The phrase:

```text
lock-in
```

describes increasing stability inside Phase 2.

It does not mean:

- Phase 2 ended
- a second Phase 2 was declared
- progression became mandatory
- Phase 2D was achieved
- Phase 3 became active

## Historical Phase 2C Language

Some reports use:

```text
Phase 2C
```

Within the canonical hierarchy, Phase 2C is interpreted as historical shorthand for:

```text
Phase 2 consolidation / lock-in observation
```

It is not treated as a separate canonical phase.

Historical reports retain their original wording.

# Transition Evidence Rules

A structural transition should be evaluated using accumulated evidence rather than one favorable observation.

Relevant evidence may include:

- artifact continuity
- structured measurement
- repeated protocol execution
- stable recovery
- preserved movement quality
- portability
- perturbation response
- biological snapshots
- model-error outcomes
- risk assessment
- governance preservation

One metric or one successful session is insufficient.

---

# Transition Entry Requirements

A future formal transition entry should record:

1. transition identifier
2. prior canonical state
3. new canonical state
4. classification
5. evidence window
6. declaration date
7. supporting artifacts
8. contradictory or incomplete evidence
9. risk assessment
10. operational consequence
11. affected repository documents
12. confirmation that the declaration is retrospective

---

# Historical Preservation Rule

Historical reports and closeout summaries should retain their original terminology.

They should not be rewritten solely to match the current canonical vocabulary.

When historical wording differs:

- preserve the original language
- interpret it through `PHASE_MAP.md`
- add a central alias or clarification
- do not infer a transition that was never formally declared
- do not classify terminology cleanup as biological correction

---

# Excluded Events

The following do not normally require a state-transition entry:

- ordinary weekly progress
- one favorable wearable reading
- short-lived fatigue
- transient bodyweight fluctuation
- one successful movement variation
- one travel event
- one recovery disruption
- routine repository maintenance
- source-artifact correction
- privacy remediation
- provider-export ingestion
- measurement-system documentation updates

These events may be important elsewhere without changing structural system state.

---

# Governance Boundary

State-transition language must not be used to:

- create progression pressure
- justify added workload
- convert candidate evidence into mandatory testing
- reward favorable outcomes
- rewrite the prior state
- conceal contradictory evidence
- alter open predictions
- declare progression prospectively

The governing sequence remains:

```text
Observe
  ↓
Preserve evidence
  ↓
Assess repeatability
  ↓
Review contradictions and risk
  ↓
Declare retrospectively
```
