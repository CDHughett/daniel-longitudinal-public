# Concepts

This document provides plain-language definitions for recurring concepts used throughout the Daniel Longitudinal Study archive.

---

## UDI

UDI (Uncertainty Delta Index) is a framework used to evaluate how well predictions align with observed outcomes.

The purpose of UDI is not to eliminate uncertainty.

The purpose is to measure how uncertainty changes as observations accumulate.

---

## Model Error

A model error is a documented difference between what was expected and what actually occurred.

Model errors are treated as learning opportunities rather than failures.

Each error becomes evidence that can be used to improve future predictions.

---

## Prediction Auditing

Prediction auditing is the process of comparing a prediction against a later observed outcome.

The archive records both successful and unsuccessful predictions.

The objective is calibration rather than validation.

---

## Concordance

Concordance refers to the degree of agreement between prediction and outcome.

Higher concordance suggests the model is describing reality more accurately.

Lower concordance suggests revision may be required.

---

## Collection Layer

The collection layer captures observations.

Examples include:

- wearable data
- body weight
- biomarker results
- training logs
- recovery observations
- daily reports

The collection layer records events as they occur.

---

## Archive Layer

The archive layer organizes observations into durable records.

Examples include:

- weekly reports
- audit reports
- model error records
- findings
- longitudinal summaries

The archive layer preserves patterns rather than individual moments.

---

## Telemetry

Telemetry refers to information collected from measurements, observations, and recorded outcomes.

Telemetry provides the evidence used throughout the archive.

---

## Artifact-First

Artifact-first means observations are documented before interpretation.

Measurements, reports, and records are preserved as artifacts.

Interpretation is performed after evidence exists.

---

## Recovery Floor

Recovery floor describes the lowest functional state reached during disruption.

Improvement may occur through a higher recovery floor, faster recovery, or both.

---

## Phase Transition

A phase transition is a meaningful shift in system behavior.

Examples may include changes in recovery characteristics, training capacity, prediction performance, or methodological development.

Phase transitions are identified retrospectively through accumulated evidence.
