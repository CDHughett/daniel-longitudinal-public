# For Observers

This is the broader observer-review path for the Daniel Longitudinal Study.

For a compact technical inspection route, see:

→ [`OBSERVER_QUICKSTART.md`](./OBSERVER_QUICKSTART.md)

This repository is not:

- a product
- a coaching system
- a completed scientific framework
- a generalized protocol
- a clinical study
- a performance-marketing archive

It is an active single-subject longitudinal archive under continuous observation, retrospective interpretation, and explicit model calibration.

---

## What You Are Looking At

The archive is organized into intentionally separated evidence and interpretation layers:

- **source artifacts** → provider reports, testing outputs, exports, images, and other primary evidence
- **structured data** → machine-readable longitudinal values derived from governed sources
- **active reports** → contemporaneous collection during an open observation window
- **closed reports** → retrospective interpretation after the applicable window closes
- **model-error layer** → registered predictions compared with later observed outcomes
- **phase and state documents** → retrospective classification of longer operating patterns
- **governance** → correction, prediction, protocol, and interpretation constraints
- **dashboards** → compressed current-state and derived views
- **audits** → repository-integrity and governance reviews

Each layer has a different purpose.

The archive should be evaluated partly by whether those roles remain distinguishable.

## Governing Distinctions

### Observation Versus Interpretation

Observation may occur during an active window.

Examples include:

- training-session notes
- wearable measurements
- bodyweight
- pain or GI state
- movement-quality observations
- environmental context
- candidate evidence relevant to an open prediction

Interpretation of the completed weekly or biological window remains retrospective.

An active report may preserve evidence.

It should not present the unfinished interval as a resolved outcome.

---

### Planning Versus Prediction

Operational planning may be forward-looking.

Examples include:

- scheduling a test
- identifying observation targets
- defining collection conditions
- preserving a protocol
- planning an audit
- identifying evidence needed for later evaluation

A physiological or performance prediction belongs in the governed model-error layer.

Registered predictions must be:

- recorded before the relevant outcome is known
- time-bounded or state-bounded
- specific enough to evaluate
- preserved without hindsight revision
- evaluated after sufficient evidence exists
- allowed to remain open when evidence is insufficient

The archive prohibits unregistered forward certainty.

It does not prohibit all future-oriented planning or formally registered prediction.

---

### Candidate Evidence Versus Outcome

An observation may be relevant to an open model-error record without resolving it.

Candidate evidence may be documented when:

- it was contemporaneously observed
- its relevance is stated
- uncertainty is preserved
- the underlying prediction remains unchanged
- the governing evaluation threshold has not yet been met

Candidate evidence does not automatically:

- pass a prediction
- fail a prediction
- close a record
- justify a phase transition
- authorize protocol progression
- become a final conclusion

---

### Correction Versus Rewriting

The archive permits narrow, source-backed correction.

Examples include:

- transcription errors
- incorrect dates
- mislabeled metrics
- broken references
- inaccurate source attribution
- structured values that conflict with verified source evidence

A valid correction should:

- identify the affected content
- rely on the strongest available source
- change only supported material
- preserve unrelated valid evidence
- retain the prior state through Git history
- document material effects
- trigger review of dependent summaries

The archive prohibits:

- silent alteration
- unsupported reconstruction
- outcome-driven rewriting
- deletion of unfavorable valid evidence
- revision of a prediction after the outcome is known

---

## System Rules

The current governing rules include:

- source evidence precedes interpretation
- structured data remain traceable to their sources
- active observations remain provisional
- completed windows are interpreted retrospectively
- forward predictions remain isolated in the model-error layer
- unregistered forward claims are prohibited
- candidate evidence does not determine an outcome by itself
- unfavorable valid outcomes remain visible
- missingness remains visible
- source-backed corrections remain traceable
- phase declarations occur retrospectively
- protocol progression requires repeated evidence and governance support
- ordinary-life conditions are not represented as laboratory control
- dashboards may compress evidence but may not strengthen claims beyond their sources
- audits constrain correction without rewriting the evidence being audited

---

## Environmental-Control Boundary

The archive documents one subject under:

- defined protocol constraints
- broadly stabilized recurring inputs
- repeated measurement
- incomplete real-world environmental control

Relevant ordinary-life variability may include:

- travel
- schedule displacement
- household workload
- social or family demands
- altered sleep environment
- hydration and sodium variation
- meal and GI timing
- illness or irritation
- recreational activity
- equipment access
- measurement timing
- device or provider behavior

The archive should not be read as a laboratory experiment.

Its strength depends on transparent context, repeated observation, and bounded interpretation rather than perfect environmental isolation.

---

## Why This Exists

Many self-tracking systems collapse several distinct activities into one narrative:

- measurement
- memory
- interpretation
- prediction
- intervention
- identity
- public presentation

When those layers collapse, data can become story and story can become evidence.

This archive attempts to reduce that risk by separating:

- what was captured
- what was transcribed
- what was contemporaneously observed
- what was retrospectively interpreted
- what was predicted
- what later occurred
- what was corrected
- what remains unresolved

The archive does not attempt to eliminate error.

It attempts to preserve enough structure that error can be identified, classified, and used to improve the model.

---

## What to Expect

Observers should expect:

- evolving structure
- incomplete historical coverage
- stronger recent structure than early structure
- a mixture of source artifacts, structured data, and narrative evidence
- conservative interpretation
- explicit uncertainty
- registered predictions
- visible model error
- ordinary-life perturbations
- ongoing methodology refinement
- no claim that one subject generalizes to others
- no claim that one intervention explains every outcome
- no expectation that every open question resolves cleanly

The system is judged primarily by:

- continuity
- traceability
- governance
- correction quality
- prediction accountability
- consistency between evidence and interpretation

It is not judged only by whether the subject improves.

## How to Evaluate Active Reports

Active weekly reports may contain:

- starting-state context
- observation targets
- current constraints
- protocol boundaries
- contemporaneous measurements
- training notes
- candidate evidence
- unresolved questions

An active report should be treated as a collection surface.

Observers should ask:

- Is the observation clearly dated or placed within the active window?
- Is the value direct, transcribed, derived, or subjective?
- Is candidate evidence labeled as provisional?
- Does the report avoid closing an unfinished trajectory?
- Does it preserve the original prediction?
- Does it distinguish planning from outcome interpretation?

The presence of contemporaneous observations in an active report is not itself a governance failure.

The relevant question is whether the archive preserves their provisional status.

---

## How to Evaluate Closed Reports

Closed reports may provide retrospective synthesis of:

- training continuity
- recovery behavior
- sleep
- bodyweight and fluid context
- environmental constraints
- perturbations
- mechanical observations
- model-error relevance
- governance outcomes

Observers should ask:

- Was the observation window actually closed?
- Are numerical summaries consistent with the structured data?
- Are important metric distinctions preserved?
- Are unfavorable signals included?
- Are missing or noncomparable values acknowledged?
- Does the interpretation remain proportional to the evidence?
- Are candidate future-phase characteristics kept distinct from confirmed phase transitions?
- Does later evidence appear to have rewritten the original window?

A report should organize evidence rather than replace it.

---

## How to Evaluate Snapshots

Snapshot review should consider:

- source identity
- collection date
- provider or device
- preparation conditions
- checksum presence
- comparability with earlier snapshots
- missing pages or fields
- structured transcription accuracy
- algorithm or software changes
- whether the artifact is primary, derived, or redacted

A valid checksum confirms that a file matches the archived digest.

It does not establish:

- clinical validity
- assay accuracy
- causal interpretation
- privacy appropriateness
- comparability across providers
- correctness of every provider-generated conclusion

---

## How to Evaluate Structured Data

Structured data should be inspected for:

- consistent schemas
- units
- date continuity
- duplicate dates
- missingness
- transcription errors
- field-label consistency
- semantic plausibility
- agreement with source artifacts
- direct versus derived status

A CSV may parse successfully while still containing a semantic error.

Observers should therefore distinguish:

- syntactic validity
- continuity
- semantic consistency
- source verification
- measurement validity

Known unresolved issues are recorded in:

[`../data/DATA_QUALITY_NOTES.md`](../data/DATA_QUALITY_NOTES.md)

## How to Evaluate Predictions

The model-error layer should be evaluated according to whether predictions are:

- registered before outcomes
- sufficiently specific
- preserved after registration
- evaluated under predefined rules
- left open when evidence is insufficient
- retained when incorrect
- distinguished from operational planning
- prevented from driving proof-seeking protocol behavior

Observers should inspect:

- the original prediction record
- its registration date
- the observation window
- known information at registration
- evaluation criteria
- contemporaneous evidence
- source outcome artifacts
- closure notes
- error classification
- downstream calibration updates

A correct prediction is not proof that the model is generally correct.

An incorrect prediction is not a reason to remove the record.

---

## Model Errors

A model error occurs when observed reality differs materially from the registered expectation.

The archive should preserve:

- what was expected
- what occurred
- whether the model overestimated or underestimated
- whether the record was evaluable
- what evidence supported closure
- whether future rules changed

Model error is useful when it improves:

- calibration
- threshold selection
- uncertainty handling
- intervention governance
- future prediction quality

The system is stronger when it can preserve a wrong expectation without narrative repair.

---

## UDI

The Uncertainty Delta Index is used to evaluate changes in model uncertainty across eligible records.

Observers should not treat UDI as a biological score.

It is a model-calibration construct.

Its usefulness depends on:

- consistent eligibility rules
- preserved prediction records
- valid outcome classification
- transparent exclusions
- separation of open and closed records

Open or non-evaluable records should not be forced into UDI calculations merely to create completeness.

---

## Concordance

Concordance describes agreement between prediction and observed outcome.

Higher concordance may indicate that the current model is describing the tracked system more accurately.

Lower concordance may indicate:

- model error
- inadequate thresholds
- poor comparability
- missing evidence
- unstable conditions
- an incorrectly framed prediction

Concordance is not certainty.

It is one calibration signal.

## What a Skeptical Observer Should Inspect

A skeptical review should examine whether:

- source artifacts remain accessible
- structured data agree with sources
- missingness is visible
- active reports preserve provisional status
- closed reports remain retrospective
- registered predictions are separated from ordinary interpretation
- wrong or incomplete predictions remain visible
- model-error records are not rewritten
- unfavorable valid outcomes are retained
- corrections are source-backed and traceable
- dashboards do not introduce stronger claims
- phase declarations follow explicit criteria
- protocol progression is evidence-governed
- data coverage is represented accurately
- limitations are stated where they matter
- current documentation agrees across major governance surfaces

The archive is strongest when it can be inspected from several directions and still produce the same evidence hierarchy and boundary conditions.

---

## Warning Signs

Potential warning signs include:

- an outcome appearing without a source
- a structured value changing without correction documentation
- a prediction becoming more vague after the result
- an active report presenting a completed conclusion
- a dashboard making a stronger claim than the underlying report
- unfavorable measurements disappearing
- missing data being replaced by zero or inference
- a phase transition declared from one event
- protocol progression introduced to force a snapshot result
- ordinary-life observation described as complete environmental control
- a retrospective summary creating events absent from contemporaneous records
- administrative complexity increasing without improved traceability

Such findings do not automatically invalidate the archive.

They should trigger targeted review.

---

## Public Archive Boundary

The public repository may not contain every privately retained source artifact.

Public evidence may include:

- original reports
- redacted derivatives
- structured values transcribed from privately retained sources
- reports grounded in verified nonpublic material
- contextual documentation sufficient for review

Observers should distinguish:

- public absence
- source absence
- missingness
- deliberate privacy reduction
- noncomparability

A privately retained source may support a public structured value, but the limitation should remain visible when external verification is reduced.

---

## Scope Boundary

This archive documents one subject under defined protocol constraints and incomplete environmental control.

It does not:

- establish population-level conclusions
- provide a protocol recommendation
- offer medical advice
- prove clinical efficacy
- identify one cause for every change
- guarantee future outcomes
- establish that consumer-device measurements are clinically definitive
- convert personal observations into universal claims
- treat prediction accuracy as proof of authority

Its appropriate use is as a governed longitudinal case archive.

---

## Observer Checklist

A concise review can ask:

1. What is the claimed current state?
2. Which source artifacts support it?
3. Which values are structured?
4. Which observations are narrative?
5. Is the relevant report active or closed?
6. Are forward claims formally registered?
7. Are candidate observations still provisional?
8. Are data-quality issues visible?
9. Are corrections traceable?
10. Are unfavorable outcomes preserved?
11. Are environmental limitations acknowledged?
12. Are phase claims proportional?
13. Are open predictions still open?
14. Does the dashboard match the underlying report?
15. Can the interpretation be traced back to evidence?
