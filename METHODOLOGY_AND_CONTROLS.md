# METHODOLOGY AND CONTROLS

## Overview

This repository prioritizes longitudinal stability and control discipline over rapid optimization or intervention novelty.

This repository documents an ongoing longitudinal N=1 human performance and longevity experiment.

The objective is not rapid optimization or short-term intervention outcomes, but the observation and documentation of long-duration biological adaptation under controlled lifestyle conditions.

This project emphasizes:
- Reproducibility
- Structural consistency
- Long-term biological response
- Transparent documentation of protocol evolution

All data, reports, and snapshots are organized to allow external observers to understand methodology, assumptions, and constraints.


---

## Study Design

**Study Type:**  
Longitudinal self-experiment (N=1)

**Subject:**  
Male, age 36 at study initiation

**Duration:**  
Multi-year continuous observation (ongoing)

**Primary Aim:**  
Evaluate the effects of consistent training, recovery discipline, nutrition stability, and lifestyle regulation on biological aging trajectory and functional performance.

## Controlled Variables (Stabilized Conditions)

The following domains are intentionally maintained within narrow ranges to reduce confounding variables:

- Consistent wake/sleep scheduling
- Stable training frequency
- Structured aerobic base work
- Fixed core supplementation protocol
- Consistent nutritional patterns
- Regular audit cadence
- Standardized data entry procedures

These controls allow observed changes to be interpreted within a stable environmental framework.


---

## Intervention Philosophy

Changes are introduced conservatively.

Protocol adjustments occur only when one or more conditions are met:

1. Sustained adaptation plateau
2. Recovery capacity increase
3. Objective biomarker feedback
4. Structural training phase transition

Short-term optimization cycles are intentionally avoided.


---

## Versioning and Change Control

All meaningful protocol modifications follow repository version discipline.

A change qualifies for documentation when it affects:

- Training structure
- Supplement protocol
- Measurement methodology
- Recovery strategy
- Data interpretation framework

Minor day-to-day variability does not constitute protocol change.

Major updates are reflected in:
- CHANGELOG.md
- Dataset annotations
- Snapshot documentation


---

## Drift Monitoring Framework

Potential negative trajectory drift is monitored using upstream indicators:

- Sleep architecture disruption
- HRV compression trends
- Persistent fatigue signals
- Connective tissue stress feedback
- Loss of execution consistency

Corrective action prioritizes recovery and stability rather than escalation.


---

## Reproducibility Considerations

This project distinguishes between:

### Likely Transferable Factors
- Training consistency
- Sleep discipline
- Aerobic base development
- Progressive overload management
- Recovery prioritization

### Individual-Specific Factors
- Genetic variability
- Prior athletic history
- Psychological adherence traits
- Environmental context

Results should therefore be interpreted as a documented case study rather than universal prescription.


---

## Data Integrity Practices

To maintain longitudinal validity:

- Historical data is never retroactively altered
- Corrections are appended rather than replaced
- Measurement sources are preserved
- Audit trails remain public where possible

Monthly and periodic audits verify structural integrity.


---

## Limitations

This repository represents a single-subject observational framework.

Limitations include:
- Absence of randomized controls
- Environmental variability
- Individual biological uniqueness

Interpretation should prioritize trend direction rather than isolated measurements.


---

## Intent

The long-term intent of this repository is to provide a transparent, structured reference documenting sustained human biological adaptation over time.

The project prioritizes continuity over novelty and stability over rapid optimization.
