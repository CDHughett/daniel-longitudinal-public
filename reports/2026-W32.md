## Autonomic-Performance Divergence Observation

Week 31 established a meaningful separation between recovery telemetry and demonstrated functional capacity.

Week 32 showed that this separation was dynamic rather than fixed.

The early week contained:

- lower daily HRV
- lower sleep HRV
- higher resting heart rate
- higher sleeping heart rate
- a pronounced overnight cardiovascular disturbance

while:

- mood remained calm
- GI state remained stable
- B1 remained available
- Load Integration remained available
- movement quality remained intact
- initiation remained preserved

The second half of the week contained:

- higher daily HRV
- higher sleep HRV
- lower resting heart rate
- lower sleeping heart rate
- improved subjective sleep continuity

while:

- B1 remained ambient
- Load Integration remained ambient or strong
- movement quality remained intact
- initiation remained preserved

The most important observation is therefore not that better autonomic values produced better functional behavior.

The functional phenotype changed very little.

The physiological layer moved substantially around a comparatively stable behavioral layer.

This argues against a simple one-to-one readiness interpretation in which a particular HRV or heart-rate value directly determines same-day usable capacity.

It also argues against ignoring recovery telemetry because performance remains strong.

The wearable data detected a physiological state change that the behavioral layer alone would have missed.

The behavioral data prevented that physiological state change from being misclassified as immediate global dysfunction.

Both layers were necessary.

The first observed reconvergence occurred through the autonomic layer improving toward the preserved functional layer rather than the functional layer deteriorating toward the compressed autonomic state.

That is the central Week 32 finding.
