## Purpose

This report closes the W41 ordinary-home observation window. It retains the **original question from the active W41 opening**, before its outcome was known:

> Does the newly increased, explicitly recorded LI workload remain mechanically controlled, behaviorally inexpensive and recovery-compatible when repeated under ordinary conditions?

The W40 retrospective descriptor was **controlled expansion**: the initial registered pull/press and hinge/grip increases were tolerated at low reported overall effort, with standard, reduced-load and LI rest boundaries preserved. W41 was intended to examine repeatability rather than automatically increase the prescription further.

The completed W41 record supports **conditional repeatability**. Seven B1 and six Load Integration sessions were completed, the scheduled LI rest day was respected, and operator-reported training and ordinary-day function remained favorable. But the October 14 pull/press exposure did **not** simply replicate W40's 5×4 pull-up prescription: it advanced to 5×5. October 17 more closely repeated the prior hinge/grip prescription. Selected overnight heart-rate elevations and shorter nights preserve uncertainty about cumulative recovery cost.

The private Week 36 / 2026-W41 operator narrative uses the descriptor *conditional repeatability*. This public report summarizes evidence from the governed structured source and preserves relevant limitations; the private narrative is interpretive context, **not independent source verification and not a public artifact**.

Subjective vivid and sometimes semi-lucid dreams cannot verify or disprove these stage-duration estimates, and neither stage average independently establishes recovery adequacy.

## Governance Review and Retrospective Interpretation

W41 is **not** an unrestricted higher-dose capacity test. The pull/press variant increased again, while hinge/grip more closely replicated W40's increased volume. Both appeared subjectively manageable, and the scheduling architecture preserved standard work, lower-dose work and LI rest without compensatory accumulation.

The public evidence favors **repeatable behavioral execution and favorable short-window functional availability**. It does not establish a permanently larger capacity ceiling, verified absence of accumulated musculoskeletal fatigue, the etiology of variable sleeping heart rate, or nutritional sufficiency under ongoing progression.

Relative to the opening question, the defensible answer is conditional:

- **Mechanical and behavioral availability:** Favorable by the operator's contemporaneous reports and completed session record, with brief added attention during increased pull-up work.
- **Recovery compatibility:** Favorable immediate functional follow-up, but incompletely resolved across the week because sleep and cardiovascular recovery markers varied.
- **Dose comparability:** Hinge/grip received a nearer repeat of the W40 prescription; pull/press increased further, so it was not an exact same-dose replication.
- **Governance:** Preserved. A lighter session remained lighter and a scheduled LI rest day remained free of formal resistance training.

The retrospective Week 41 descriptor is therefore **conditional repeatability**: greater-than-baseline work was performed again within retained boundaries, while its ongoing physiological cost and the next safe progression increment remain open questions.

This interpretation is stronger than an anecdote of unused reserve, and narrower than proof of established higher-dose capacity.
