## Purpose

This report closes the complete ordinary-home observation window following Week 39's short-interruption **retention** result. The starting question was whether the established B1 + Load Integration architecture would remain low-overhead for seven ordinary home days without travel-transition effects. That opening question was subsequently extended by bounded, documented resistance-training progression exposures.

The week demonstrated an uninterrupted aerobic anchor, six completed LI sessions of different prescribed doses, and a deliberately preserved LI rest day. Two LI sessions increased selected demand without a reported immediate mechanical or functional penalty. The result supports **favorable first-exposure tolerance of controlled expansion**, not proof of repeated-dose recovery, maximal reserve, nutritional sufficiency, or a formal Phase 2D transition.

The private operator narrative describes this week as *controlled expansion*. This public report retains the falsifiable exposure record and its limitations without treating that narrative as an independent measurement source.

Its evidence is usable as a first-exposure observation, with causal and attribution limits preserved. The bounded change does not automatically authorize another increase.

## Weekly Interpretation

Week 39 established that ordinary training remained callable after a temporary interruption. Week 40 demonstrated that the retained architecture could absorb **specified additional LI demand on first exposure** while keeping B1 intact and preserving a deliberately lighter day and a resistance-training rest boundary.

The two progression days produced a real change in prescribed dose. Pull-ups increased from 16 to 20 repetitions and push-ups from 30 to 36 on the pull/press day, with supported rows introduced. On the hinge/grip day, trap-bar repetitions increased from 15 to 20 at the same load and total hang duration increased from 90 to 135 seconds. The operator reported overall RPE around 4, preserved movement control and no immediate loss of daily functional availability.

The evidence does **not** show maximal capacity, a validated dose-response relation, repeated-dose recovery, connective-tissue adaptation, or diet sufficiency. Favorable HRV does not independently settle those questions, especially with several shorter nights. Observed lower bodyweight remains compositionally unexplained.

The retrospective descriptor **controlled expansion** captures the observed transition without elevating it into a phase declaration. More work was performed on named days; governance was retained on the other days.
